# Criterion information about repeatable overall impressions

The estimand is the expected Fisher information in one criterion rating about a proposal's standardized latent overall merit within the US sample that supplied the rating. Each of the four complete criteria is examined on the same within-sample overall-grade scale. Ratings preserve native categories. Better ratings occupy the upper tail of the latent variable. A one-unit change represents one between-proposal standard deviation of reviewer-assessed overall merit in that sample. The US samples come from different competitions, rubrics, times and applicant pools.

## Calibration across reviewers

Let a continuous underlying overall response for reviewer j of proposal i be standard normal with conditional mean sqrt(rho) × theta_i and residual variance 1 − rho. The common proposal effect theta_i has mean zero and variance one. Under exchangeability, the correlation between continuous overall responses by two different reviewers of the same proposal is rho. We estimate rho with the primary ordering model's function `ordering_fit`. For criterion c, let lambda_c be the correlation of its continuous response with theta_i. Its residual variance is 1 − lambda_c². The criterion response may share an impression with the same reviewer's overall response. Calibration pairs the criterion from one reviewer with the overall rating from a different reviewer of the same proposal. Under the model their underlying-response correlation is lambda_c × sqrt(rho). Dividing this cross-review correlation by sqrt(rho) identifies lambda_c.

This model assumes invariant ordered thresholds and a common linear normal relationship over the merit range. The distinct-reviewer errors must be conditionally independent of one another in the criterion–overall pairs after conditioning on the shared proposal effect. Shared information, prior discussion, reviewer assignment and systematic differences between competition strata can affect this interpretation. An assessor's criterion-specific perspective and any stable aspects of a proposal orthogonal to its overall merit enter the criterion's residual variance. The data support prediction of independent reviewers' impressions. Evaluation of scientific impact would require external outcomes.

Thresholds are inverse normal quantiles of proposal-weighted empirical cumulative category frequencies. Each proposal gets weight one, divided equally across its available reviews. For each criterion, the correlation maximizes the composite log likelihood of bivariate normal rectangle probabilities for all distinct ordered reviewer pairs. Each multiply reviewed proposal gets total weight one, divided equally among its pairs. Singleton NIH proposals inform marginal thresholds. Category thresholds accommodate unequal frequency and heaping on each native scale. For the distinct-reviewer correlation this procedure adapts the two-step polychoric likelihood. [Olsson, 1979](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02296207)

For a criterion with ordered cuts b_k and residual SD s = sqrt(1 − lambda²), the probability p_k(theta) of category k equals the difference between the normal distribution functions at (b_k − lambda × theta)/s and (b_{k−1} − lambda × theta)/s. Single-rating information is the sum over categories of [p'_k(theta)]²/p_k(theta). Each curve concerns one rating. Joint information requires the dependence structure of all four criterion ratings. We evaluate the central 80% of the model's reference distribution in each sample. The 90th percentile is a model reference position with sparse direct tail support in AIBS (72 proposals in total).

## Uncertainty and diagnostics

The 999 seeded bootstrap samples each resample whole proposals with replacement within a dataset. Every draw re-estimates all marginal thresholds, both-reviewer overall correlation and four cross-review associations. The intervals in Figure 2 use the pointwise 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. At the selected upper reference position, the six pairwise contrasts have simultaneous intervals *within each dataset*. For each bootstrap draw we calculate all six differences, subtract their respective full-sample estimates, divide each deviation by the bootstrap SD of its contrast and take their maximum absolute value. The 95th percentile of that maximum supplies the common multiplier for full-sample differences ± multiplier × bootstrap SD. All 999 fits supplied finite admissible curves for every criterion in both samples. The code flags boundary or unidentified anchor/loading estimates. The intervals condition on the sampled competitions and rely on resampling proposals as exchangeable clusters. Repeated appearances of the same reviewer across different proposals can induce additional dependence.

At the 90th percentile, NIH Approach exceeds the other three shared criteria in all three simultaneous contrasts. AIBS Innovation has the highest point estimate and all six simultaneous contrast intervals include zero. AIBS influence checks refit after removing each reviewer and each review year. Innovation leads in 22 of 26 reviewer omissions and two of three year omissions. Approach leads in the other four reviewer omissions and Significance leads in the other year omission. These checks retain the fitted assumptions about the relationship between grades and latent merit.

An additional predictive check holds out entire proposals in five folds. In each training set we fit the ordered grade margins and cross-review associations. They give two probabilities that a second reviewer will assign an overall grade of 2 or better, conditional on whether the first reviewer placed the proposal at grade 2 or better on a single criterion. The baseline uses the training-set prevalence of an overall grade of 2 or better. We calculate each test proposal's mean squared probability error (Brier loss) across all distinct reviewer pairs and average with equal proposal weights. New proposals can be reviewed by assessors who also appear in the training folds. On NIH the largest gain over baseline is .0047 for Approach. On AIBS the largest gain is .0145 for Innovation. The event uses native categories and has different prevalences across the two datasets.

## Reproduction

Run these commands from the repository root after preparing the US files.

```sh
Rscript --vanilla scripts/criteria_checks.R
Rscript --vanilla scripts/criteria_analysis.R
Rscript --vanilla scripts/criteria_sensitivity.R
Rscript --vanilla scripts/criteria_figure.R
```

The analytic checks compare category probabilities with the primary model, compare Fisher information with finite differences, and recover a known criterion loading in simulated proposals containing a large shared impression specific to a reviewer and proposal. The simulated loading is .60 and the recovered estimate is .597. `results/criteria_curves.csv` contains all displayed curve values, bounds and loadings. `results/criteria_contrasts.csv` supplies the upper-reference comparisons. `results/criteria_prediction_checks.csv` and `results/criteria_aibs_influence.csv` reproduce the diagnostics. The plotting script uses the publication theme adapted from TGI. [TGI source attribution](../docs/tgi-reuse.md)

Lines show estimates across the 10th to 90th percentiles of standardized latent overall merit within each sample. Shading gives pointwise 95% proposal-bootstrap intervals.
