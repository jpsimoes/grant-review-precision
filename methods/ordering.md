# Overall-rating precision

The estimand is the probability that the native mean of three ratings favours the proposal higher in latent reviewer-assessed merit. The two proposals differ by a fixed number of within-pool proposal standard deviations. They receive independent sets of three ratings. Tied means receive half credit. NIH and AIBS enter as distinct historical samples. The nine SNSF panels or sections each supply a separate calibration. The four Project Funding sections provide the main Swiss comparison. The five smaller Postdoc.Mobility panels describe fellowship applications selected for panel discussion.

## Measurement model

Within each pool, let the latent proposal effect θ and rating residual ε have independent standard normal distributions. The latent response for one rating is

    Z = sqrt(ρ) θ + sqrt(1 − ρ) ε.

Pool-specific thresholds convert Z to the observed overall grade. This model represents dependence between votes on a proposal through a shared proposal effect. Stable reviewer severity, reviewer–proposal interactions and common panel deliberation can also affect the recorded grades. The fitted signal reflects features shared by the proposal's ratings, including shared discussion in the Swiss settings. The observed data support an interpretation in terms of historical reviewer judgement. The latent variable has its own unit in each pool.

Native score increments are preserved, including the observed tenth-points in AIBS. Scores are oriented toward greater merit for ranking and converted back to native direction for reporting. AIBS categories that were never used have zero estimated mass. Each proposal gets equal weight in the marginal grade distribution, with that weight split across its observed reviews. Marginal cumulative frequencies determine the ordinal-probit thresholds. The positive dependence parameter ρ maximises the bivariate ordinal composite likelihood over [0, 0.999]. Each proposal with two or more ratings contributes a total weight of one across all distinct within-proposal reviewer pairs. The model predicts probit-scale differences and grade agreement under this historical mixture of raters. Reviewer assignment and grade distributions would have to generalise to support prospective use.

## Fixed-gap ordering and score translation

For a percentile u and gap δ, the two proposal effects are Φ⁻¹(u) − δ/2 and Φ⁻¹(u) + δ/2. At each location, compute the multinomial probabilities of the three-rating sum by convolving the single-rating native-score distribution three times for each proposal. Integrate over all pairs of these discrete sums to obtain the probability of a correct ordering with ties split equally. The primary gap is 0.5 SD. Gaps of 0.25 and 1 SD test sensitivity to the size of the merit difference.

The expected native overall grade at each midpoint is the inner product of the conditional category probabilities and the native grade values. This adapts the expected-score grid in the TGI-SR+ study to the fitted probit model. Fisher information for three independent ratings is three times the sum of squared probability derivatives divided by their respective category probabilities. The exported information is conditional on fixed parameters and a standardised proposal effect in the specified pool.

## Uncertainty and cutoffs

Proposal-cluster intervals use 400 replicates with independent unit-mean exponential weights per proposal, refitting the margins and ρ each time. Within-proposal pair dependence is preserved. A two-way sensitivity multiplies proposal weights by independent exponential weights for the reviewer identifiers and renormalises ratings and distinct-rater pairs within each proposal. The reported 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles are pointwise, model-conditional uncertainty bands. These reweightings measure sensitivity to the observed proposal and reviewer mix. Discussion effects and the chances of a repeat independent panel remain model assumptions.

The midpoint grid runs from the 1st to the 99th percentile in one-percentile steps. For a pool of size n, both members of each pair must lie between percentiles 1/(n+1) and n/(n+1). These are normal-model plotting positions. A stricter sensitivity requires at least five expected proposals beyond either member of the pair. Actual latent locations are unobserved. The corresponding checks use model-implied percentiles and pool size.

For each δ, each support rule and each accuracy target of 0.65, 0.70, 0.75, 0.80, 0.85, 0.90 or 0.95, I examine the supported upper half of the grid. A terminal crossing requires a location at or above target followed by an interval that stays below through the end of this grid. Linear interpolation between the last point reaching target and the next point gives the estimated crossing. I separately record curves that stay below throughout, curves that meet the target throughout and curves with dips followed by recovery. The fraction of multiplier replicates with any terminal crossing and the location interval conditional on such a crossing are both reported. Upper bounds and crossing locations depend on the evaluated grid. A target expresses an illustrative decision preference. A native grade at a crossing is a model-based expectation for a proposal at that latent location.

## Predictive checks

For each fit, compare exact within-proposal agreement and mean absolute grade difference under the empirical pair distribution and the fitted joint distribution. A deterministic-seed five-fold split holds out whole proposals. Draw one trio of reviews for each held-out proposal with at least three reviews. Predict each member of the trio from the other two on all three rotations. The model integrates over the latent proposal location with 41-node Gaussian quadrature. The comparators are the training-set mean and the mean of the two observed grades. Native-scale root mean squared errors use the same held-out targets for all three methods. Targets whose two predictors include a grade absent from the training fold are omitted and counted. Reviewer identifiers may recur across folds. The task checks calibration on other proposals scored under the same procedures.

For an upper-range calibration check, pool the out-of-fold predictions within each pool. Assign predictions at or above their empirical 75th percentile to the upper group, including all ties. Within each group, report the mean predicted and observed native third grade and the predicted and observed frequency of the best grade. The three targets from each trio can contribute to different groups. Counts of targets and distinct proposals are included. The grouping uses predictions from the two available grades and therefore supplies a check at the favourable end of predicted scores.

Run `Rscript --vanilla scripts/ordering_analysis.R` and `Rscript --vanilla scripts/ordering_verify.R` from the repository root. The first script reads the prepared long data and writes four aggregate CSV files under `results`. The second checks a known bivariate-normal threshold model, compares the discrete convolution against direct triple enumeration and checks accuracy against the corresponding optimal continuous-score upper bound.
