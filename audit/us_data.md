# NIH and AIBS review data

Run `Rscript --vanilla scripts/us_prepare.R` from the repository root with the R packages `digest` and `igraph`. It fetches and verifies the `NIH.rda` and `AIBS.rda` files shipped with ShinyItemAnalysis 1.5.1, prepares one row per reviewer–proposal assignment, and reproduces the adjacent CSV audit tables. Source URLs and SHA-256 digests are in `us_sources.csv`. The script checks unique assignment keys, observed score bounds and increments, completeness of the four shared criteria, and agreement between reconstructed proposal means and the distributed overall means.

## Review designs

NIH comprises 5,802 preliminary R01 reviews of 2,076 distinct applications submitted by Black and White PIs, sampled from council years 2014–2016. Ratings run from 1 (best) to 9 (worst). The public file spans 200 scientific review groups, 24 integrated review groups and 22 administering institutes. It lacks a meeting or round identifier for reconstructing an actual payline. A pooled top-percentile analysis therefore describes the sampled cases. NIH has five criterion scores. The 2,499 review rows with a missing final panel score correspond to the label `ND` (not discussed). Preliminary overall scores are complete. [NIH data dictionary](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/ShinyItemAnalysis/html/NIH.html) · [Erosheva et al. study and sampling design](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaz4868) · [original materials](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/4D6RX)

The AIBS data contain three assigned reviewers for each of 72 proposals from a collaborative biomedical research program. The file records 21 proposals in 2014, 29 in 2015 and 22 in 2017. All 2014 proposals are classified as Standard. The latter cycles contain both Pilot and Standard proposals. Overall and criterion scores use a 1 (best) to 5 (worst) scale with tenth-point increments. Reviewers used 21 distinct overall grades and gave integers in 149 of 216 reviews. The Impact criterion is observed for all 63 ratings in 2014 and missing throughout the later two cycles. [Gallo dataset](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.12728087) · [AIBS data dictionary](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/ShinyItemAnalysis/html/AIBS.html)

The records provide overall grades and four complete, similarly named criteria in both sources. The shared domains are significance, investigator, innovation and approach/feasibility. Cross-program comparisons require respecting the local rubric and score direction. Neither review file identifies award recipients. Erosheva, Martinková and Lee previously used both datasets to demonstrate range restriction in agreement estimates. [2021 reliability study](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssa.12681) · [reproducibility materials](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/KNPH8)

## Assignment overlap

NIH proposals have between one and five observed reviews. Of 2,076 proposals, 75 have one, 368 have two, 1,544 have three, 86 have four and three have five. Of 4,555 reviewers, 3,542 appear once. A bipartite graph linking proposals and reviewers has 973 components. In 608 components reviewers link to just one proposal. The largest component connects 32 proposals. Fixed reviewer and proposal effects across these components require additional identifying constraints. A hierarchical model can use exchangeability assumptions that should be explicit. Grade comparisons and reproducibility estimates across research groups also reflect differences in case mix and reviewer assignment.

AIBS has one connected graph of 26 reviewers and 72 proposals. Three reviewers contributed a single review. Reviewers can therefore link proposals in this file, although the small sample leaves limited support for fine distinctions in the upper tail. A validation design using two assigned reviews to predict a third is possible for all 72 AIBS proposals and the 1,633 NIH proposals with at least three observed reviews. The 75 singly reviewed NIH proposals contain no within-proposal contrast.

## Derived columns

`data/derived/us_reviews.csv` contains native numeric scores with 1 denoting the best rating. Use (`dataset`, `proposal_id`, `reviewer_id`) as the assignment key and read the IDs as character. Both IDs are anonymized and specific to their dataset. `review_group`, `integrated_review_group` and `administering_institute` are NIH strata. `cycle` gives the AIBS year. `proposal_type` uses each source's original labels (New/Renewal for NIH and Pilot/Standard for AIBS). `overall` is the preliminary overall impact score in NIH and the scientific merit score in AIBS. The four shared criterion columns retain the local rating scale. `environment` is specific to NIH, and `impact` applies to AIBS in 2014. Empty cells represent unavailable measurements. Proposal and reviewer IDs have no cross-dataset linkage.
